This started in 2025, I had been using [PhotoScape X](http://x.photoscape.org/) for years, but I realized, I was not getting the most out of my camera. I realized that PhotoScape X was a 8 bit software, so it was not able to take advantage of the 14-bit RAW files from my Canon EOS 80D.

After that I started looking around for photo editing softwares, I got to watching [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l_xS1Ro9BN0) and [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NsIRKd24JA) on youtube which compared multiple softwares.

One of the primary reasons I wanted to switch away from PhotoScape X mainly because it lacked non-destructive editing.

## Softwares I Tried

### Darktable

![Darktable UI](/blog/pictures/photo-editing-3.png)

I started with the free softwares, I started with [Darktable](https://www.darktable.org/) which is open source, I had heard some great things about it, as for the results, they were great but the tools were just too confusing to wrap my head around.

### Rawtherapee

![RawTherapee UI](/blog/pictures/photo-editing-2.png)

This is an interesting one. I wanted to try this other open source alternative.

At first, I was disappointed since the preview quality was a bit blurry, I did some research and found that this is a problem more people are facing specially on MacOS. I went with it since when I zoom into the photo at `100%` it looked alright.

The export quality was even better so I stuck with it.

This is still one of the softwares I use on a regular basis.

The other thing I discovered was that I can change the lens profiles to use better quality lens profiles. I ended up downloading [Adobe DNG Converter](https://helpx.adobe.com/in/camera-raw/desktop/dng-and-file-formats/digital-negative.html), which gave me access to adobe lens profiles and used these lens profiles as dynamic rules to substitute the open source lens profiles that RawTherapee provides.

![RawTherapee Dynamic Profiles](/blog/pictures/photo-editing-1.png)
**This the dynamic profiles in Rawtherapee I have setup**

Most of these profiles are for hooking up Adobe lens profiles to Rawtherapee directly. The others are colour export settings.

RawTherapee has one feature which makes me keep coming back, which is capture sharpening. This uses the `Richardson–Lucy (RL) deconvolution`, this method helps specially with macro photography since at close distances, there is a lot of diffraction when you're at narrow apurtures and so close to your subject, `Capture Sharpening` helps to counter that diffraction.

> Richardson–Lucy (RL) deconvolution is an iterative mathematical procedure used to recover a sharp, underlying image or signal from data blurred by a known point spread function (PSF) and degraded by Poisson noise.

This is a software I still use on a regular basis. The other software I pair with RawTherapee is Topaz Photo AI.

### Topaz Photo AI

![Topaz Photo AI UI](/blog/pictures/photo-editing-4.png)

Topaz Photo AI is not exactly a photo editing software, its a collection of AI tools to enhance photos.

I use it mainly for AI Denoise and to sharpen (mainly for macro photos) my photos.

> One thing to note is that if you go overboard with the AI adjustments, the photo can look waxy or plastic-y. The key is to use it subtly.

This is another piece of software I use regularly in conjunction with RawTherapee.

Unfortunately, the new Topaz Photo has become a subscription which I am not too pleased with, the version I have is a legacy license which I bought when it was around $250 one-time I think.

### DxO Photolab 9

![DxO Photolab 9 UI](/blog/pictures/photo-editing-5.png)

DxO Photolab is one software, I saw many sponsored videos for. So I got curious and wanted to try it out.

If you are into photography, you'll probably be familiar with the company DxO for [DxO Mark](https://www.dxomark.com/) which is a camera sensor and lens performance evaluation website. They have been testing cameras and lenses for years, so naturally they have a lot of data relating to this.

DxO photo lab 9 uses this camera and lens database to provide lens corrections which are second to none in the industry since they pair camera and lens into what they call `DxO Modules`.

They also provide an algorithm called deepPRIME which does noise reduction and de-bayering. It is one of the best in the industry for noise reduction. This algorithm is tailored to your camera sensor.

> **Note**: DxO Photolab works well only if the camera and lens is in their database, it uses this camera sensor/lens data to provide sharpness corrections, etc.

DxO Photolab really impressed me with its noise reduction capabilities specially for wildlife and bird photos, its almost like it gave my camera a new life.

## What I Actually Use

### For Macro Photos

For Macro Photos I use RawTherapee since I do mostly reverse lens macro, which means that there is no Exif data available to DxO since it works best with the DxO Lens Modules which are specific to camera and lens combinations.

## Where is Lightroom?

Sadly, Lightroom never fit my use-case since it relies on a subscription model which gets more expensive every year, also the catalogue system is just not for me.

I also prefer portability and all the photo editing softwares use sidecar files which makes the edits extremely portable. This way I can rely on my filesystem to do all the organizing.

Yeah, I miss AI Search, but it doesn't really affect my workflow anyway.

> A sidecar file is a companion text file that stores adjustments, history, and metadata for an image. It sits next to the original raw photo with the same name but a different extension like `.pp3` for RawTherapee or `.dop` for DxO Photolab. This allows programs to edit images without changing the raw file.
